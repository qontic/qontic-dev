3D Dirac Packet Splitting

Explanation

This WebGPU simulation shows a free massive four-component Dirac Gaussian packet in three dimensions. A generic fixed spinor contains positive- and negative-energy spectral branches, so the packet can separate into lobes moving in opposite directions even though their central momentum points the same way.

Free Dirac wave

The wave evolves according to the free Dirac equation in the standard representation.

iℏ∂tψ=−iℏcα·∇+mc2βψ

The initial Gaussian carries a fixed spinor chosen at the central momentum as a 50/50 mixture of the +E and -E eigenvectors.

ψ∼f(x)eik0·xcosχ2u++sinχ2u−

Why opposite motion?

The two free Dirac branches have opposite dispersion slopes. For the same momentum, the negative-energy branch has the opposite group velocity.

E±=±m2c4+c2p2,vg,±=±c2pm2c4+c2p2

Bohmian guidance

The yellow particles are actual Bohmian configurations guided by the full four-spinor Dirac current.

All three spatial directions are periodic. The displayed box is one fundamental cell of the 3-torus. This is a 3D numerical analogue of the paper's 1D splitting mechanism, not a proof of the 1D theorem in 3D.
