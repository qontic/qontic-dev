1. Begin with the classical oscillator

A mass on a spring feels a restoring force that always points back toward equilibrium. In a modern physics course, this classical picture becomes the starting point for the quantum harmonic oscillator.

The potential energy is a parabola. Near the bottom of many physical potentials, the curve is approximately parabolic, which is why the harmonic oscillator appears in molecular vibrations, solids, fields, and quantum optics.

Orange: parabolic potential V(x). Green: restoring force F=−kx. Gold marker: classical mass moving between turning points.

Math means this picture

F=−kx,   V(x)=½kx²
On the plotThe orange curve is potential energy; the green arrow is the force.
MeaningThe farther the mass is from equilibrium, the harder the spring pulls it back.

Prediction check

If the spring gets stiffer while the mass stays the same, what happens to the period?
A stiffer spring produces a larger restoring force, so the oscillator completes a cycle more quickly. The period decreases.

2. The quantum oscillator is described by a wavefunction

In quantum mechanics, the object is described by a wavefunction Ψ(x,t). The plotted gold curve, |Ψ|², gives the probability density for position measurements.

MeaningThe wavefunction evolves smoothly, but a position measurement gives one location.
Why is the gold curve stationary for one energy state?

For a single energy eigenstate, the time dependence is a phase factor:

Ψn(x,t)=φn(x)e−iEnt/ℏ

The phase changes Re Ψ and Im Ψ, but it cancels out in |Ψ|². That is why the probability density of one energy eigenstate does not move back and forth like the classical mass.

3. The allowed energies form an equally spaced ladder

The quantum harmonic oscillator cannot have just any energy. Its energy levels are evenly spaced, and the lowest allowed energy is not zero.

Each horizontal line is an allowed energy. The lowest line is the zero-point energy, E₀=ℏω/2.

Math means this picture

En=ℏω(n+½),   n=0,1,2,...
Symbolsn is the energy-level number; ω sets the spacing between levels.
On the plotThe green-highlighted line is the selected energy.
MeaningEnergy comes in equally spaced steps. The oscillator cannot sit perfectly still at E=0.

Prediction check

If ω increases, what happens to the spacing between adjacent energy levels?
The spacing is ΔE=ℏω. Larger ω means larger energy steps.

4. Zero-point motion comes from confinement and uncertainty

The ground state is the lowest-energy state, but it still has a finite width and finite energy. A quantum oscillator cannot have both perfectly known position and perfectly known momentum.

ground-state |Ψ|²
V(x)
Δx marker

Math means this picture

ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2
For n=0Δx = √[ℏ/(2mω)] and Δp = √[ℏmω/2].
MeaningSqueezing the position distribution raises momentum uncertainty. The ground state balances these costs.
Connect uncertainty to zero-point energy

A rough energy estimate is

E ≈ (Δp)²/(2m) + ½mω²(Δx)²

If Δx is made too small, the kinetic-energy term grows. If Δx is too large, the potential-energy term grows. The lowest balance gives E₀=ℏω/2.

5. Superpositions can move probability density

A single energy state has a stationary density. A superposition of two energy states has a changing relative phase, so the probability density can move and breathe in time.

A coherent-state-like packet is included because it is the quantum state that most closely resembles classical simple harmonic motion: the center of the packet oscillates while its shape stays nearly fixed.

7. Orthodox and pilot-wave accounts use the same wavefunction

The calculations agree in the cases shown here. The interpretations differ in what they say about the particle between preparation and measurement.

The harmonic oscillator is especially helpful because a real stationary energy state has zero probability current. In a pilot-wave account, that means configurations are stationary even while the complex phase of the wavefunction changes.

Interpretation note

Standard account

Ψ is a probability amplitude. The Born rule says |Ψ|² gives the density of possible position outcomes. This account does not assign a definite unmeasured path between measurements.

Pilot-wave / Bohmian account

Ψ guides a definite configuration through v=j/|Ψ|². For a real stationary oscillator state, j=0, so configurations remain still. Superpositions and coherent packets can have nonzero guidance motion.
