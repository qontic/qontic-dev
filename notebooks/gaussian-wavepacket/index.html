Before you begin: three linked ideas

A quantum wavepacket connects a state description, a probability density, and possible detector events. Follow the envelope, the phase, and the repeated-measurement pattern together.

1. A packet is localized but not pointlike

Ψ(x,0)

The initial state has a center and a width. A smaller width means the position distribution is more concentrated.

2. Width in position affects momentum spread

σxσp ≥ ℏ/2

A narrow packet requires a wider range of momentum components. Those components are what make the packet spread.

3. Free evolution: the packet spreads

Even with no external force, the packet spreads because its momentum components move with different velocities. The packet center moves with the group velocity, while its width increases in time.

Free-particle width.σx(t)=σ₀ √[1+(ℏt/(2mσ₀²))²]Lighter particles and narrower initial packets spread more quickly.

5. Orthodox and Bohmian accounts use the same wavepacket

The calculated probability density is unchanged. The interpretations differ in whether an unmeasured particle is assigned a definite configuration between preparation and detection.

Trajectory account.v(x,t)=j(x,t)/|Ψ(x,t)|²For this free Gaussian, the same guidance law gives outward motion as the packet spreads.
Interpretive difference: the orthodox view uses the wavefunction to assign measurement probabilities. The Bohmian view adds definite particle positions guided by that same wavefunction.

Questions students often ask

Use these prompts to connect each visual feature with the equations above.

Does spreading mean the particle is being pushed by a force?

No. The notebook uses a free particle with no potential. Spreading comes from the packet's range of momentum components.

Why do lighter particles spread faster?

For the same initial width, the factor ℏt/(2mσ₀²) is larger when m is smaller.

Where is the particle before detection?

The orthodox account does not assign a definite unmeasured position. The Bohmian account assigns a definite configuration guided by the same wavefunction.

Why do Bohmian particles move outward?

The probability current of a spreading Gaussian points outward relative to the moving center, so configurations farther from the center separate as the packet broadens.
