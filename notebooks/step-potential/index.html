Before you begin: three linked ideas

A step potential separates space into two regions with different potential energy. The same wavefunction determines reflection, transmission, probability current, and the Bohmian guidance field.

1. The potential changes the local wavelength

V(x)

On the higher-potential side, the kinetic energy is lower when E>V₀, so the transmitted wavelength is longer.

2. A wave can reflect from a change in medium

R+T≈1

For E>V₀, a packet can partly reflect even though a classical particle would cross.

3. Below the step, the tail is evanescent

e−κx

For an infinite step with E<V₀, the wave penetrates a short distance but carries no lasting transmitted flux.

4. Below-step scattering: E < V₀

When E is below the step height, the infinite step has no propagating transmitted wave. The wavefunction penetrates into the right region as an evanescent tail, and the packet eventually reflects.

6. Orthodox and Bohmian accounts use the same scattering wave

The measurable reflected and transmitted probabilities come from the same wavefunction. The Bohmian view adds definite particle configurations whose velocities are guided by the probability current.

Why can reflection occur above the step?

A sudden change in potential changes the wave impedance of the quantum state. Matching Ψ and its derivative at the step generally produces both reflected and transmitted amplitudes.

Does the evanescent tail mean the particle crosses an infinite step?

No. For E<V₀ in an infinite step, the right-side solution decays and carries no propagating transmitted current.

What is shared by the two interpretations?

They use the same wavefunction and predict the same reflected and transmitted measurement statistics.

What does Bohmian mechanics add?

It adds a definite configuration guided by j/|Ψ|² between preparation and measurement.
