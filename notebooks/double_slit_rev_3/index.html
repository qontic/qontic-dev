Before you begin: three linked ideas

The apparatus picture, the amplitude math, and the detector record answer different parts of the same question. Follow them in that order rather than treating the equations as an extra layer added after the experiment.

1. A quantum state can have two path amplitudes

ψ=ψU+ψL

The two terms are alternatives in one state description. They are not two particles that need to collide in order to interfere.

2. Amplitudes combine before probabilities

P(x)∝|ψ(x)|²

The screen probability is calculated after the upper and lower amplitudes are added. This “add, then square” rule produces interference.

3. One run gives one dot

one detector event

A single photon gives one localized screen event. The fringe pattern is a frequency pattern built from many independently prepared photons.

3. de Broglie wavelength and photon energy

Every quantum object obeys the de Broglie relation λ=h/p. For light, p=Eγ/c, so the same relation becomes Eγ=hc/λ. Shorter wavelengths therefore carry larger photon energies.

This optical notebook uses the photon form of the de Broglie relation. A particle-mass parameter is not used because photons are massless.

A two-slit pattern is not a pure cosine. Each slit has nonzero width, so the single-slit diffraction envelope modulates the interference fringes.

6. Which-path information reduces interference

Coherence is the survival of a stable relative phase between the fields from the two slits. When that phase is randomized by environmental coupling, source bandwidth, path marking, or averaging, the interference cross term is reduced.

The coherence factor γ rescales only the interference cross term: γ=1 is fully coherent and γ=0 is the incoherent sum of two single-slit intensities. It does not change the wavelength, slit geometry, or fringe locations. This graph uses the same fixed ±20 mm detector span as Part 5, so changing λ or L visibly changes the pattern while changing γ reduces contrast.

Start with the role of the coherence factor.Pγ = PU + PL + γ(interference term)γ measures how much stable relative-phase information remains. γ=1 keeps full interference; γ=0 leaves the additive two-slit pattern without interference fringes.

7. Orthodox and Bohmian accounts use the same calculated pattern

The calculated screen probability distribution is unchanged. The interpretations differ in whether they treat an unmeasured particle as having a definite position and path.

What a trajectory account changes—and what it does not.|Ψ|² gives the screen distribution   |   v ∝ j/ρ guides a Bohmian configurationBoth accounts use the same calculated field and predict the same screen statistics in this model. The difference is interpretive: the pilot-wave account adds a definite configuration between measurements, while the standard account does not assign a definite unmeasured path.
Interpretations are not optional storytelling: they make different claims about what may exist between measurements. The observed screen statistics alone do not distinguish the two accounts in this model.
Interpretation note

Standard (orthodox) quantum account

Ψ is a probability amplitude. The Born rule assigns the screen density |Ψ|². A detection is localized, but this interpretation does not assign a definite unobserved path from source to screen.

Bohmian mechanics

Bohmian mechanics supplements the wavefunction with definite particle configurations guided by the wave. For this optical scalar-field model, the gold curves use the calculated scalar current as an optical guidance analogue. The incoming source-to-slit segments are schematic preparation paths; the post-slit curves are forward current-transport paths derived from the longitudinal scalar current. This is not a complete relativistic Bohmian photon theory.

Questions students often ask

These are conceptual guideposts. Return to the nearby panels to connect each answer with an equation and a visual result.

Which slit does the particle actually go through?

In the orthodox account used here, no definite unmeasured path is assigned. In a pilot-wave account, a definite configuration passes through one opening while the guiding wave has contributions associated with both openings.

How does a single particle “know” where to go on the screen?

It does not need a plan or a message from the screen. The state prepared by the apparatus evolves according to the wave equation, and the Born rule gives the probability for each possible detector location.

Does “observing” the slits mean a person looks at them?

No. In physics, observation means an interaction that creates a record in a detector or the environment. A reliable path record makes the alternatives distinguishable and reduces the interference term.

Is this just a classical two-stream pattern with small corrections?

No. Classical particles through two openings give an additive two-hump pattern. Quantum amplitudes combine before probabilities are calculated, producing the additional interference term and the fringe pattern.
