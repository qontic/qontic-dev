1. Start with the interferometer

A Michelson interferometer splits one light beam into two perpendicular arms, reflects the beams from mirrors, and recombines them. If the two returning waves have different phases, the detector sees bright and dark interference fringes.

The key idea is simple: the experiment compares two round-trip light travel times. The hard part is deciding what classical ether theory would predict and what nature actually does.

Cyan and blue: split light beams. Orange: beam splitter and mirrors. Gold: detector fringe signal made when the returning beams recombine.

MeaningThe detector does not see each beam separately; it sees the result of recombining their waves.

2. What would an ether wind predict?

Before special relativity, many physicists imagined light as a wave traveling through a medium called the ether. If Earth moved through that medium, light should take slightly different times along the arm parallel to the ether wind and the arm perpendicular to it.

This is the experiment’s central prediction check: a classical ether model predicts a small but measurable round-trip time difference. The modern result is that this expected shift is not observed.

Prediction check

If the hypothetical ether speed v increases, what happens to the predicted time difference?
The predicted effect grows like v². Doubling v makes the predicted time difference four times larger.

Math means this picture

Δt ≈ Lv²/c³
On the plotThe green bar is the predicted difference between the two round-trip times.
MeaningA longer arm or faster ether wind would make the classical predicted difference easier to see.
Show the round-trip time formulas

In the classical ether model, the parallel and perpendicular round-trip times are:

tparallel = L/(c−v) + L/(c+v)
tperpendicular = 2L/√(c²−v²)

For v much smaller than c, their difference is approximately Δt ≈ Lv²/c³.

3. A path difference becomes a phase difference

The detector responds to phase, not to travel time directly. A tiny path difference can shift the bright and dark fringes because one returning wave may arrive slightly ahead of the other.

For beginners, the important step is: path difference → phase difference → fringe shift. This is the same wave logic used in two-slit interference, but now the two paths are the interferometer arms.

Prediction check

How much path difference returns the same fringe pattern?
A one-wavelength path change adds 2π of phase, returning the same bright-dark pattern.

Math means this picture

Δφ = 2πΔℓ/λ
On the plotChanging Δℓ shifts the gold fringe pattern left or right.
MeaningOne wavelength of extra path corresponds to one full phase cycle.

4. Rotate the apparatus and look for a fringe shift

If the ether-wind picture were correct, rotating the interferometer should swap which arm is more parallel to the wind. The predicted time difference would change with angle, producing a shift in the fringes.

The crucial signature is not simply “there are fringes.” Fringes exist because the instrument is an interferometer. The predicted ether signal is a change in fringe position as the apparatus rotates.

5. The null result: what was not seen?

The experiment did not find the fringe shift expected from motion through a stationary ether. This result challenged the idea that light needed a mechanical medium with a preferred rest frame.

A “null result” does not mean nothing happened on the screen. It means the specific predicted change—an orientation-dependent ether-wind fringe shift—was absent within experimental sensitivity.

6. The modern relativity reading

Special relativity replaces the ether-wind expectation with a new principle: the speed of light in vacuum is the same for all inertial observers. The Michelson-Morley result is one important experimental clue that this older classical picture needed revision.

Historically, several ideas were proposed to explain the null result. In modern teaching, the experiment is often used to motivate why “relative to the ether” is not needed in the laws of physics.

Careful historical note

The experiment alone is not the entire derivation of special relativity. It is one important result, together with Maxwell’s equations and other evidence, that made the old ether-wind picture difficult to keep.

The Lorentz-contraction idea was historically introduced before Einstein’s 1905 interpretation. Special relativity later made length contraction and time dilation consequences of spacetime structure rather than mechanical effects of motion through an ether.

7. What conceptual difficulty did the experiment resolve?

The Michelson-Morley experiment is valuable because it turns a philosophical-sounding question—“What is light moving relative to?”—into a measurable prediction. The missing fringe shift helped move physics away from a preferred ether frame.

Use the selector to compare three accounts.

Classical ether account

Light is treated like a wave in a mechanical medium. Motion through that medium should create an orientation-dependent travel-time difference.

Experimental lesson

The expected orientation-dependent fringe shift was not found. The measurement challenged the idea of a detectable ether wind.

Modern relativity account

The laws of physics do not require a preferred rest frame for light. Observers in inertial frames measure the same vacuum light speed c.
