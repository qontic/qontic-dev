Orbitals and motion

These are three energy eigenstates of nonrelativistic hydrogen with a fixed proton. In each one, the probability density |ψ|² stays constant in time. The teal dots sample that density. The gold dot shows one possible position and its pilot-wave trajectory.

What sets the trajectory?

In the spin-free model, the guiding velocity is v = (ħ/mₑ) Im(∇ψ/ψ). The 1s and 2p₀ orbitals can be taken real apart from their overall time phase, so their guiding velocity vanishes wherever the density is nonzero. Their probability clouds have different shapes even though both particles stay still.

The 2p₊₁ state has spatial phase eiφ. Its particle goes around the z-axis at fixed height and fixed distance ρ from that axis. Its angular speed is ħ/(mₑρ²). The example starts at the same position for all three orbitals.

Model and scale

This lesson uses the scalar Schrödinger current. Including electron spin adds a spin-current term that can produce motion even in 1s. The distance bar uses picometers (pm), and the clock uses attoseconds (as). Playback slows the atomic motion so you can see it.
