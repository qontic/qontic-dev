Physics and model limits

What you see

The app opens in a 3D view; the XZ button gives the side view, with the beam moving to the right along x and z up. The cloud is the total density of the two-component spinor:

ρ(x,t) = Ψ†Ψ

The dots are pilot-wave particles. They start at random positions drawn from |Ψ|² and then follow the guidance law below. Yellow dots start above the packet centre, green dots below. A dot stops when it reaches the detector plate, and the counts in the corner are made from those dots. The wave itself passes through the plate and keeps evolving.

The default packet is an equal, in-phase superposition of +z and −z spin. In the field the two spin components separate vertically. Each particle ends up in one branch, and which one is fixed by where it started: dots from the upper half of the packet go up, dots from the lower half go down. Only dots that start very near the middle can end in the other branch.

Two readings

The interpretation button in the controls switches between two readings of the same wave. In the pilot-wave reading the particle has a position at all times and is guided by the wave; the dots show possible positions. In the orthodox reading only the wave is shown: the theory gives the particle no position before it is measured, and |Ψ|² gives the probabilities of where it will be found at the plate. For the equal superposition each half of the plate gets probability ½; for +z the upper half gets nearly all of it. Both readings predict the same statistics at the plate. In the orthodox reading the box in the corner shows the weight of |Ψ|² above and below the middle of the box; when the packet reaches the plate, these are the chances of a hit in each half.

Geometry

The packet starts inside the magnetic field. There is no entrance or exit of a magnet: this is a close-up of the region inside it. The grid is periodic along x, so the box is drawn as copies placed end to end, and the cloud is shown in the copy where the packet is. The detector plate is two box lengths from the start of the first box. The grid is twice as tall along z as along x and y. Along y and z the walls are hard; with the default settings less than 2% of the density is near the top and bottom walls when the packet reaches the plate. Larger gradients push the branches into the walls, so the gradient slider stops at 3.5.

Magnetic field

The field has no x component and does not depend on x:

B=g (0,−y′, 2+z′) , y′=y−ycL , z′=z−zcL

Here yc and zc are the centre of the box, L is half the box height, and g is the gradient strength, so ∂Bz/∂z = g/L. The uniform part, 2g, keeps the field from vanishing inside the box, so the spin components separate without spin flips. The y component is required by ∇·B = 0; it has almost no effect here. The red lines are field lines of this B: at every point the line is tangent to B, and the arrowheads (Display: Field arrows) show which way B points along it. The lines carry no strength by their colour; the strength shows in their spacing, since the lines crowd together where |B| is larger, toward the top. That crowding is across y, so it is seen in the 3D and YZ views but not in the XZ view, where every line is seen edge-on as a vertical stroke. The Magnetic field switch (Display tab) turns the whole field off, uniform part included.

Wave

The particle is neutral, so there is no vector potential. The spinor obeys the Pauli equation

iℏ∂tΨ = - ℏ22m ∇2 - ℏ2m σ·B Ψ

With this sign the magnetic moment is parallel to the spin, μ = (ħ/2m)σ, and the +z component is pushed toward the stronger field, upward. For a silver atom the magnetic moment of the outer electron is antiparallel to its spin, so there the −z component would go up.

The equation is solved with fourth-order finite differences in space and leapfrog steps in time, periodic along x and with hard walls along y and z. The initial packet is a Gaussian of width σ (Packet width) moving along x; its wave number is rounded to the nearest value that fits the periodic box.

Guidance

The particle velocity is the Pauli current divided by the density. It has a convective part and a spin part. The convective part is

vconv = jconvρ
jconv = ℏm Im (Ψ†∇Ψ)

and the full velocity is

v = jconvρ + sℏ mρ ∇× (Ψ†σΨ)

The coefficient s = ½ gives the Pauli current. The spin term is divergence-free, so every value of s keeps the dots distributed as |Ψ|²: the counts at the plate have the same statistics, and only the individual trajectories change. With s = ½ the trajectories of a +z packet also swirl around the beam axis; this is the spin current, not a numerical error. The controls use simulation units: ħ = 6, m = 1 and grid spacing 1.
