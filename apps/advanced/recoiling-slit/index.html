What controls the fringes?

The photon and atom become entangled. Each possible photon path is associated with a different recoil state of the atom. When those states overlap strongly, interference is strong. When they can be distinguished reliably, interference is weak. Reading the recoil is not required for this loss of visibility.

The model

There are two equally weighted paths A and B. The atom begins in a pure Gaussian momentum state with standard deviation σₚ. The interaction shifts its mean momentum to −Δp/2 or +Δp/2. Both recoil states have the same width and no relative momentum-dependent phase.

|Ψ⟩ = (|A⟩|a⟩ + eiφ |B⟩|b⟩) / √2

For recoil separation r = Δp/σₚ, the state overlap sets the fringe visibility:

V = |⟨a|b⟩| = exp(−r²/8)
P(D₊) = ½(1 + V cos φ),   P(D₋) = 1 − P(D₊).

The output probabilities are those of a balanced recombiner. A phase scan gives an interference fringe at each output. This is an interferometer fringe, rather than a spatial double-slit pattern on a screen.

Recoil readings and path information

The colored curves are momentum probability densities conditional on path A or B. Their horizontal axis is u = p/σₚ; each curve integrates to one. A single momentum reading generally cannot identify a path with certainty. The measured atom distribution, when both photon outputs are included, is the equal mixture of the two curves. Gray histogram bars estimate that mixture and are normalized by the number of recorded atom readings.

Photon detections and atom readouts are sampled from their joint quantum probability, rather than drawn independently. If fA(u) and fB(u) denote the two Gaussian densities,

P(u, D±) = ¼ [ fA(u) + fB(u) ± 2√(fA(u)fB(u)) cos φ ].

Turning on the readout starts recording atom data from that point. It is an ideal later measurement of momentum, after the recoil interaction; it adds no new interaction with the photon. No path label is assigned to an individual event.

Scope

This is a pure-state, one-dimensional recoil model with a balanced, lossless interferometer. It excludes atomic heating, mixed motional states, optical losses, finite collection angle, trap evolution, background counts, and detector imperfections. The recoil slider directly controls Δp/σₚ; it is not a calibrated trap-depth control. Momentum is displayed in units of σₚ, and the apparatus is not drawn to scale.

The simulation shows shared quantum predictions. It does not distinguish orthodox quantum mechanics, pilot-wave theory, and many worlds. The colored recoil curves represent quantum states, rather than classical particles choosing paths.

From a thought experiment to a single atom

Einstein proposed using a movable slit to infer a photon’s path from its recoil. Zhang and colleagues realized a related interferometer using a single atom in an optical tweezer as a quantum beam splitter. Changing the trap depth changed the atom’s momentum uncertainty and the visibility of single-photon interference.

Y.-C. Zhang et al., “Tunable Einstein-Bohr Recoiling-Slit Gedankenexperiment at the Quantum Limit,” Physical Review Letters 135, 230202 (2025).

A useful question for students

“Why do the fringes weaken even when nobody looks at the atom?” A good explanation should refer to entanglement and recoil-state overlap. The experiment does not require a conscious observer.
