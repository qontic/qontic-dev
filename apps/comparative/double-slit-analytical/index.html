Quantum Double-Slit Experiment: Why it Matters

The double-slit experiment is one of the clearest demonstrations that quantum systems cannot be understood as ordinary classical particles moving through one slit or the other. When both slits are open and no which-path measurement is made, single particles gradually build up an interference pattern. When a which-path detector is introduced, that pattern disappears.

This simulation lets you compare three different representational views of the same experimental situation. In the Collapse view, the wavefunction evolves and then detection events are sampled from the probability density. In the Pilot-Wave view, particles follow definite trajectories guided by the wave. In the Many-Worlds view, the wave continues to branch into multiple outcome components rather than collapsing.

These views agree on the basic detection statistics, but they suggest very different microscopic stories about what is happening between preparation and measurement. The purpose of the simulation is not to decide between them, but to make the representational differences visible.
