Math

Each initial packet has Gaussian density with standard deviations σₓ and σᵧ. Free evolution is exact for this prescribed initial state.

g(q,t) = (2πσ²)−¼(1+iτ)−½ exp[−(q−q₀−kt)² / (4σ²(1+iτ)) + ik(q−q₀) − ik²t/2],   τ=t/(2σ²)
Ψ(x,y,t) = gₓ(x,t) [gᵧ(y+d/2,t)+gᵧ(y−d/2,t)] / √[2(1+exp(−d²/(8σᵧ²)))]
v = Im(∇Ψ/Ψ),   Pᵢ = ∫₀ᵀ dt ∫pixel i dy jₓ(L,y,t)

Gaussian density width grows as σ(t)=σ√[1+(t/(2σ²))²]. Particles start from |Ψ(x,y,0)|², without sampling screen endpoints. Longitudinal trajectories are analytical; transverse trajectories use fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration. The predicted screen probabilities use numerical quadrature of the analytical current, not a numerical wave solver.

Rationale and limits

This first packet mode prepares one Gaussian at a single exit, or a coherent pair at two exits, then propagates freely. It does not yet model an incoming source packet or scattering from a hard slit wall. The initial Gaussians have tails on both sides of the preparation plane.

Each pulse represents independently prepared, noninteracting particles with the same one-particle state. A displayed dot is one member of that ensemble; it is not an additional particle in a many-body wavefunction. Pulses are separated by a fresh preparation after 12 time units, with statistics retained.

The detector records first crossings of x=24. This is a specified crossing model, not a microscopic absorbing-detector calculation. The cyan prediction integrates outward current through the finite screen over the same pulse duration. Late particles and crossings beyond its vertical extent are reported separately. This preview currently provides Pilot-Wave trajectories; Orthodox and Many-Worlds packet detection views will require a separate integration step.

The continuous-wave app is preserved. Its pre-packet source is saved in the version 38 rollback branch.
